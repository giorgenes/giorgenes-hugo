Most founders build the product they wish existed — not the one their users need.

## The assumption trap

By the time they've written 10,000 lines of code, they've also baked in a hundred assumptions: about onboarding flow, pricing model, the "one core feature" that will change everything.

The problem isn't moving fast. The problem is moving fast in the wrong direction.

> "The riskiest thing a startup can do is build something nobody asked for. The second riskiest is building it beautifully."

## What actually kills MVPs

After reviewing dozens of failed early-stage products, the patterns are surprisingly consistent:

- Solving a problem the founder has, not the customer
- Over-engineering auth, settings, and "nice to haves" before validating the core loop
- Skipping user interviews because "we already know who our users are"
- Launching too quietly — treating stealth as a strategy instead of fear

## The fix: validate before you build

Before writing a line of code, answer these three questions with real evidence:

```js
const validate = {
  problem:  "Can you name 5 people who have this problem right now?",
  solution: "Will they pay for a solution — or just say they will?",
  channel:  "Do you know exactly how to reach them at scale?",
};
```

Shipping is still the goal. But shipping something small that proves the riskiest assumption — that's a real MVP. Everything else is just an expensive prototype.
