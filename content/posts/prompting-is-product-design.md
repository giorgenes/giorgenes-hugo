Every time you write a prompt, you are writing a product spec for a very literal junior engineer who never pushes back.

## Clarity is the job

The best PMs I know write requirements that leave no room for misinterpretation. The best prompts I've seen do the same thing — they define context, constraints, output format, and success criteria before asking for anything.

## The user is the model

In product design, you learn to think from the user's perspective — their mental model, their vocabulary, their goals. In prompting, the "user" is the model. Understanding what it has been trained on, what patterns it recognises, and where it fails is exactly the same skill applied to a different medium.
